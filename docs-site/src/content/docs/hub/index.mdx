## AI visibility for security teams

Most teams can't answer three questions: which AI tools are people using, what data are they sending, and is any of it customer or regulated data? Hub answers them from the traffic itself, not from surveys.

- **Every AI service, recognised.** Shadow's [catalog of 288 AI services](/shadow/reference/providers/) covers model APIs, inference clouds, routers, coding agents and chat apps.
- **Every app.** Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, the OpenAI SDK, Chrome, Safari and more, named on each request.
- **Every machine.** Laptops and servers enrolled with one command, with the person they belong to.

## Shadow AI detection

Shadow AI is the AI tools people use without IT knowing. Hub lists **newly seen AI services** each week and who uses them, and flags services Shadow recognises but can't protect yet (for example tools with their own binary protocols) as *seen, not protected*, so nothing AI-bound goes by unnoticed.

## Per-user insights: masked, blocked, policy violations

Open any person or machine to see the same dashboard for just them, then any incident in full:

- what was caught (names, emails, card numbers, national IDs, secrets, hostnames, ...) and what the AI received instead;
- which requests were blocked by policy (for example image uploads);
- the app, process, model and AI service;
- the prompt, as written and as sent, when your sharing level includes it.

Hub ranks people by the sensitivity of what they sent, and shows which data types go to which AI services, so you can fix the process, not just the person.
